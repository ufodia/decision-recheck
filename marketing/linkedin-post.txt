Your product changed. Your buying advice, campaign copy and support answers may still describe yesterday.

I built Decision Recheck to make those mismatches inspectable.

The fictional example starts with a $9/month Starter plan that includes CSV export. Starter rises to $29; export moves to the $49 Pro plan. The buyer's budget stays at $20.

Fixing the ad does not fix that buyer's budget.

The tool keeps three views of the same scoped evidence: product owner, buyer/user, and marketing/support. It produces local HTML/JSON bundles with linked artifacts and an explicit recheck against a saved baseline.

The latest update tightens how AI can contribute to the report.

Four optional LLM roles in the local runner select and prioritize allowed review items. The application supplies the exact recorded decisions and required follow-ups.

• A recorded "fails" cannot be softened into "ready."
• Behavior verification stays a required precondition for changing documented facts.
• Missing or untested work remains visible.
• Unknown, incomplete or stale selections are rejected.

I tested this four-role flow against HTTPX's actual public documentation with fresh OpenAI responses: a baseline and recheck, four new responses each. The checks covered documentation and browser text observations; they did not execute HTTPX's runtime behavior. The full project suite passed 335 tests.

FreshCtx supplies the dependency and freshness layer. Playwright powers scoped browser checks. These checks support reviewable decisions; they do not predict customer demand or purchase behavior.

The hosted demo is a fictional, key-free walkthrough. Live model review runs locally with your own provider configuration.
